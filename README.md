## 	What real problem does AI solve in this industry?

In sports, one of the greatest advantages of AI is its ability to generate personalized training recommendations based on an athlete's progress and current fitness level. Instead of following a one-size-fits-all program, AI can adjust weekly training plans by considering previous workouts, recovery status, injury history, and performance goals. It can also detect signs of fatigue or overtraining, recommend recovery days, and provide suggestions to reduce the risk of injury. This enables athletes to train smarter rather than simply training harder, leading to long-term improvement and more consistent performance.

As a running coach and founder of Eightensity Active Sport, I believe AI should be viewed as a tool that enhances coaching rather than replaces it. While AI can process data quickly and provide valuable insights, it cannot replace the human qualities of motivation, empathy, experience, and personal connection that coaches bring to their athletes. By combining AI-powered analytics with professional coaching, we can create safer, more effective, and highly personalized training programs that help athletes achieve their goals while building healthier and more active communities.

## 	What data would a system need to solve it?

Artificial intelligence (AI) can significantly improve the way coaches design and manage training programs by analyzing large amounts of athlete data. To create personalized training plans, AI requires information such as training load, workout intensity, consistency, recovery status, injury history, heart rate, sleep quality, and overall performance trends. By continuously collecting and analyzing these data points, AI can recommend appropriate training adjustments based on an athlete's current condition rather than relying on a fixed training schedule. For example, if an athlete misses several training sessions due to work, illness, or other commitments, AI can automatically modify the remaining workouts to maintain steady progress while reducing the risk of overtraining or injury.

Another valuable application of AI is monitoring an athlete's psychological well-being. Coaches can use simple questionnaires to assess stress levels, motivation, fatigue, and overall mental readiness. By analyzing these responses over time, AI can identify patterns that may indicate burnout, excessive stress, or declining motivation. Instead of spending hours manually reviewing assessments, AI can quickly summarize the results and suggest possible interventions or support strategies. However, AI should complement—not replace—the role of a coach. The insights generated by AI become most effective when combined with human empathy, experience, and meaningful communication. Together, AI and coaches can provide personalized motivation, emotional support, and evidence-based training that help athletes stay healthy, overcome challenges, and remain committed to achieving their long-term goals.

## What could go wrong if the system is biased, wrong, or misused — and who would that affect?

Artificial intelligence (AI) is only as reliable as the data it receives. If the data collected are inaccurate, incomplete, or biased, AI may generate incorrect assessments and recommend inappropriate training programs. For example, if an athlete provides incorrect information about their training load, injury history, recovery status, or fitness level, the AI may prescribe workouts that are either too difficult or too easy. An overly demanding program can increase the risk of fatigue, overtraining, and injury, while a program that is too conservative may result in slow progress and underperformance.

Another concern is the misuse of AI-generated training programs. Some athletes may believe they can improve faster by following the program designed for another athlete, especially one who is more experienced or performs at a higher level. However, every athlete has unique physical abilities, training history, recovery capacity, and personal goals. A program that is effective for one individual may be unsafe or ineffective for another. This can lead to unnecessary injuries, frustration, and setbacks in performance.

For these reasons, AI should be used as a decision-support tool rather than a replacement for professional coaching. Coaches play an essential role in validating the data, interpreting AI-generated recommendations, and considering factors that AI may not fully understand, such as an athlete's motivation, lifestyle, and emotional well-being. Combining accurate data, AI insights, and human expertise leads to safer, more personalized, and more effective training programs.
